# Conflict data inventory and assessment

**Reviewed November 26, 2026.** This inventory covers the historical extracts, the saved source files and the datasets described in the project handoff. The primary source manifest passed its checksum verification. Row counts below refer to the source unit shown. Case rows, victims, claims, filings, source observations and derived actions should be counted separately.

## Gaza benchmark

The saved Berman et al. paper describes daily UN Department of Safety and Security reports for 2007–2018, with the analysis window June 15, 2007 through December 15, 2017. Reported attacks on both sides have occurrence timing up to five-minute precision, location, attacker and target, weapon type and quantity, and observed deaths and injuries. The paper derives expected damage from attack characteristics and casualty outcomes, then constructs actions and response opportunities. Its example SITREP includes incidents with explicit no-harm outcomes. Comparable data need a credible observation window to interpret apparent nonresponse, in addition to a clock and two-sided actor coverage. [Paper, sections 3–5](https://eklor.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/estebanklor/files/drc-july26.pdf) · [SITREP example](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/estebanklor/files/drc_08dec2024_full_draft.pdf#page=17)

The handoff says the collaborator has the Gaza expected-human-damage data and border-crossing records. This checkout and its source directories contain the published paper and supporting material. The underlying Gaza incident, expected-damage and crossings tables were not located in the workspace, so their join, coverage and coding could not be independently audited here.

## Vietnam

**Files held.** The combined VietnamWarData release, four constituent releases (SITRA, VNDBA, TIRSA and VCIIA), four technical guides, the NARA OPSANAL manual, the release construction paper and archival finding aids. The 638,760 combined rows have distinct release identifiers. The four larger constituent tables overlap and use different reporting units. The cleaned incident table is in the cloud workspace. [Sources](sources.csv) · [Audit](audits/vietnam-audit.json) · [Tidy schema](tidy-data.md)

**Timing and actors.** The combined table gives 555,653 usable within-day clock fields and 83,107 day-only rows. Of the usable clocks, 366,243 are on rows coded Enemy and 188,599 Friendly, with 811 lacking the initiating side. These are U.S. military source classifications. 274,794 of the 535,053 timed rows with a populated source place share that exact place string, date and clock with another row. These are ordering ties and review candidates. The reported clocks lack a harmonized timezone and have not been verified as occurrence times across all constituent files. The extract contains 1,281 rows dated January 27, 1973 or later, 1,241 with usable clocks. Only six are dated after March 12, 1973, and all six fall outside the documented SITRA period.

**Damage and validation.** The extract preserves friendly and enemy deaths and injuries. Both sides' fatality fields are populated on 254,226 rows. Almost 294,000 rows lack a standardized event type. Civilian casualty fields, property loss, mobility restrictions and economic damage are absent in the standardized table. The primary battalion journal scan covers January 1–15, 1968. The fully transcribed January 1 has 37 numbered items and two summaries. Another 32 entries were reviewed across three later pages. Combat message clocks can be receipt times. The spot check linked zero journal messages definitively to an event in the release. The Nixon post-ceasefire report and Vietnamese Cửa Việt accounts are additional evidence, with aggregation and timing caveats.

**Use and missing records.** The combined release supports the strongest bulk two-sided timing feasibility study in the inventory, with scope for candidate linkage and clock validation. Acquisition of original NARA INCDA files and codebook, and access to ICCS field reports, would be needed to assess the 1973–75 pilot. The INCDA counts in the archive describe separate incident, damage and force tables. The original records were not retrieved. [Journal pilot](pilots/README.md#vietnam) · [Full source day](subdaily/README.md) · [Post-ceasefire source review](subdaily-source-search.md#vietnam)

## Iraq

**Files held.** The Dataverse replication source CSV, accompanying paper and metadata, plus a standardized, redistributable extract in GitHub. There are 251,845 rows from November 1, 2003 through December 31, 2011. The earlier DoD file described as having 253,286 observations and a standalone codebook were not present in the downloaded replication package. [Source audit](audits/iraq-audit.json) · [GitHub data](../data/iraq-incidents.csv.gz)

**Timing, actors and measures.** All records have an occurrence timestamp string and the two source date fields agree on the day. There are 509 midnight values, only eight clocks with nonzero seconds and 60,489 rows sharing a date and clock with another record somewhere in Iraq. Published reporting definitions and incidents with tied or midnight values still need validation before within-area ordering. The categories are dominated by 141,395 explosive hazards and 109,087 enemy actions. Force and attack-on fields describe roles or targets inconsistently, so the standardized initiator field is empty. The data include IEDs found or cleared, direct and indirect fire, and location and target fields. They supply no casualty totals, measured damage or mobility restrictions. Three duplicate payloads were retained. The source has 1,389 missing serials and no repeats among populated serials in the standardized file.

**Use and missing records.** Ready for descriptive analysis of recorded attacks and hazards. Two-sided response curves require coverage of coalition and Iraqi government initiated actions and their consequences. Timestamp volume alone does not recover that missing observation process.

## Colombia

**Files held.** Four CNMH case workbooks, four corresponding victim workbooks and the methodology. The tidy case table contains **94,450 unique source cases**: 39,507 military actions, 22,472 civilian-property cases, 31,971 kidnappings and 500 attacks on communities. All 95,791 victim rows link to one of 54,003 cases, using a within-source identifier. The original Spanish victim fields are preserved in the separate table. There are 13,633 populated related-case identifiers. Cross-category groups may have overlapping accounts or outcomes requiring reconciliation. [Source audit](audits/colombia-audit.json) · [Tidy schema and linkage](tidy-data.md)

**Timing, actors and measures.** There are 90,647 valid calendar dates, 998 month-only records, 865 year-only and 1,940 unknown. The bulk tables have no occurrence clocks. Cases with valid dates span October 1945 through January 2026. The standardized files have a responsible-party field on 47,076 records and a responsible-party or initiative-context field on 78,360. Broad initiative categories and suspected responsibility require different interpretations. The workbooks carry victim and injury counts, property descriptions, kidnappings and indicators for movement restriction and extortion. Positive restriction and extortion counts are 662 and 764 in files with those measures. These are case indicators and descriptions, with no consistent monetary loss or coercion intensity.

**Subdaily evidence.** The Bojayá and Mitú pilot holds 15 source observations with 10 clocks or intervals. A second CNMH Bojayá extraction has 15 observations, 11 with a clock or bound and four mapped back to the earlier pilot. The Bojayá church explosion has approximate source estimates of 10:45 and 11:00. The CINEP 2002 archive contains 506 pages and 4,045 detected case headings. The parser flags 753 fragments with clock-like text. A manual review yielded eight occurrence clocks or bounds. February 2017 MM&V publications and a November 2016 communiqué yield nine action rows across seven cases. Two cases establish action order without clocks. The Defensoría supplement has three records, two with a time of day. These samples have different periods and sources and can include overlapping observations. [Episode pilots](pilots/README.md#colombia) · [CINEP audit](subdaily/yemen-colombia/README.md#colombia) · [MM&V cases and Defensoría extract](subdaily/yemen-colombia/alternate-records.md#colombia-public-mmv-case-records)

**Use and missing records.** Strongest breadth of available damage and coercion dimensions, and usable selected episodes for testing reconciliation and chronology. Continuous subdaily ordering and day-to-day observation coverage are still absent. The original MM&V verification annex for 54 minor and 10 serious violations was not located in the downloaded closing report or monthly reports. The existence and precision of its occurrence times are unknown. [Acquisition status](subdaily/yemen-colombia/archive-acquisition.md)

## Yemen

**Files held.** The Yemen Data Project source CSV and methodology and a GitHub extract with 25,054 coalition air raids from March 26, 2015 through March 31, 2022. A raid may contain several strikes. There are 14,753 usable hour codes and 10,301 day-only records in the standardized extract. One undated row was excluded and four repeated IDs retained. Target labels and reported civilian casualty counts are available. Target labels do not quantify physical or economic loss. [Raid audit](audits/yemen-audit.json) · [GitHub data](../data/yemen-incidents.csv.gz)

**Hudaydah pilot.** Four UN-circulated coalition submissions yield 2,746 distinct claims about Houthi activity, 2,744 with usable minute values across all 104 pilot days, December 18, 2018 through March 31, 2019. Some cases describe harm or damage. The UN distributed these submissions and the claim attribution remains the coalition's. The common-window raid extract adds ten coalition air raids, eight with an hour code. It covers a different mix of actions than the claims, which include ground activity. The independent Mwatana archive adds 59 cases on 37 dates involving both sides, with 36 AM/PM labels and no occurrence clocks. No same-district date combines different attributed actors in AM and PM. Four additional UN-derived records describe damage or humanitarian access, including a scheduled convoy movement that did not proceed. Its planned 10:00 departure is not an observed incident clock. [Hudaydah claims](subdaily/yemen-colombia/README.md#yemen) · [Mwatana and humanitarian documentation](subdaily/yemen-colombia/alternate-records.md#hudaydah-december-18-2018-through-march-31-2019)

**Use and missing records.** Useful for auditing a continuous claim series, comparing sources and describing air raids and humanitarian harms. Original UNMHA or predecessor monitoring and liaison logs remain outstanding. Until comparable observations of actions by the other side and reporting coverage are obtained, combining the extracts risks treating source imbalance as strategic behavior. [Acquisition status](subdaily/yemen-colombia/archive-acquisition.md)

## Cyprus

**Files held.** The public UNFICYP report S/11353 and all 33 addenda are indexed. The curated table has 40 observations across seven reports, 18 with occurrence clocks or bounds. A subsequent source review retrieved the 681-page Turkish Red Crescent compilation and indexed 33 dated headings, plus the 632-page Greek parliamentary inquiry. The observations describe multiple actors under a common UN reporting umbrella, firing, movement and relief activity, infrastructure damage and casualties. Some reports state observed quiet intervals. [UN report extract](subdaily/README.md) · [Turkish and Greek source review](subdaily/multilingual-sources.md#cyprus)

**Use and missing records.** Strongest candidate for a common-observer pilot. Reporting windows vary and selected incidents are summarized. One addendum says UNFICYP logged 29 incidents over five hours without enumerating them. The 202 clock-like index lines also include meetings and other report times. The original observer SITREPs are outstanding, including the July 15–August 17, 1974 item S-0903-0003-03-00001 and the August 19, 1974–April 22, 1975 item S-0903-0003-02-00001.

## Bosnia and Herzegovina / former Yugoslavia

**Files held.** The UNPROFOR archival finding aid is saved. We reviewed leads for Canadian intelligence Volumes 5–6, UN Sector Sarajevo daily reports and the UN Sarajevo study and ICTY judgments. The Galić judgment cites a UN investigation that bounds a February 5, 1994 event to 12:10–12:15. The Canadian volumes and original Sarajevo situation reports are not in the workspace. The UN inventory identifies scanned Sector Sarajevo daily reports for February 17–April 9 and May 17–July 21, 1994 as Confidential. There is no incident extract. [Source assessment and file references](subdaily-source-search.md#follow-up-on-the-other-conflicts)

**Use and missing records.** Promising for common-observer coverage of shelling, sniping, civilian harm and humanitarian movement. Current evidence establishes selected clocks and archival paths. Actor attribution, continuity and nonfatal coverage cannot yet be assessed across the pilot.

## Northern Ireland

**Files held.** The committed project manuscript documents **426 preliminary measurement records** in v0.2: 262 monthly fatality observations, 13 selected chronology observations, five county-level border records and 146 archival source cells, 143 of them numerical. The latter cover searches, charges, intimidation-related housing applications, fatalities and injuries in annual or quarterly reporting. The separate v0.2 table was not located in the workspace. The NIRI incident collection and original British military daily reports, Watchkeeper logs and headquarters diaries have not been acquired here. [Manuscript, Northern Ireland application](../Multidimensional_Deterrence_Theory_Northern_Ireland.md#empirical-application-northern-ireland) · [Handoff tasks](collaborator-tasks.md)

**Use and missing records.** The documented series supports descriptive comparisons and checks against an eventual incident dataset. Fatality victim category cannot identify an initiator. Searches and charges measure state activity, and blocked roads measure restrictions rather than economic loss. The reported county total of 105 blocked crossings differs from the narrative total of 101. The evidence does not supply continuous, subdaily records of actions by both sides.

## Lebanon

**Files held.** The handoff reports an existing daily humanitarian truck-dispatch series for 2006. No copy or row count was located in the workspace. UNIFIL and humanitarian reports are identified source leads. No series of underlying reports or structured incident table was located. [UNIFIL document archive](https://www.un.org/unispal/document-source/united-nations-interim-force-in-lebanon-unifil/) · [ReliefWeb country archive](https://reliefweb.int/country/lbn)

**Use and missing records.** A validated truck series could measure daily humanitarian access. Day-level dispatch totals cannot sequence opposing actions, and the handoff gives no independently checked coverage for stops, denials, arrivals, military incidents or within-day events.

## Syria

**Files held.** SOHR daily reporting and SNHR are identified leads. No downloaded bulk export, continuous daily archive, clock audit or project incident extract was located. [SOHR daily reports](https://www.syriahr.com/en/category/sohr-statistics/daily-report/) · [SNHR](https://snhr.org/)

**Use and missing records.** Coverage, attribution and time precision remain unverified. A casualty register by itself would omit many launches, misses and nonfatal coercive actions.

## Falklands / Malvinas

**Files held.** The Argentine Ministry of Defence historical report was saved. Selected British and U.S. reporting was identified and compared in the research notes. The sources distinguish event time from later message receipt. Approximate reported clocks for the Sheffield incident differ by about ten minutes and may mark different phases. There is no structured incident table or continuous common-observer series. [Argentina and cross-source checks](subdaily-source-search.md#other-conflicts)

**Use and missing records.** Useful for testing cross-source timing and receipt clocks on individual engagements. The short campaign and selected documentation limit its present value for repeated-conflict equilibrium work.

## Locations and handoff coverage

- `data/` in GitHub holds the Iraq and Yemen bulk extracts. The reviewed transcriptions, source registers, audits and reproducible cleaning code are in `research/` and `scripts/`.
- The cloud workspace holds original publisher files at `/workspace/deterrence-source-files`, standardized extracts at `/workspace/deterrence-incidents`, Vietnam and Colombia tidy tables at `/workspace/deterrence-tidy`, and source PDFs, HTML, OCR queues and the Mwatana extract at `/workspace/deterrence-subdaily-sources`. Bulk Vietnam and Colombia data and Mwatana rows remain local while data redistribution terms are unverified.
- The handoff describes an additional 586-record multicountry v0.1 table, Northern Ireland v0.2, the latest project manuscript PDF and earlier documentation. The uploaded project Markdown is present. No copy of those separate tables or the project manuscript PDF was located, so the review does not count their rows as acquired data or assume their overlap with later extracts.

None of the available non-Gaza files supports a Gaza-equivalent response-curve estimate as currently assembled. Vietnam has the strongest bulk two-sided timing evidence, Colombia the broadest measured dimensions, and Cyprus the best public example of a common observer. Yemen's minute-level claims supply a useful target for checking against a genuinely comparable opposing series or the original monitoring records.
