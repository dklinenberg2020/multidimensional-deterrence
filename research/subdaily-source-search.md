# Subdaily archival sources for conflict sequencing

The inclusion criterion is enough information to order incidents within a day. A clock may refer to an event, an observer seeing it, the receipt of a report, or the issue of a summary. Keep those separate. Preserve approximate times as ranges when possible, retain reported time zones, and leave tied or overlapping events unresolved. These spot checks establish source potential. They do not establish completeness or retaliation.

## Colombia

- **Bojayá and Vigía del Fuerte, April–May 2002. Verified incident clocks.** The government Unidad para las Víctimas chronology records combat resuming at 06:00 on 2 May, a FARC projectile at 10:30, and the church explosion at 11:00. The preceding day's fighting is reported to have ended around 18:00. Defensoría humanitarian resolution No. 12 independently supplies an approximate clock for a paramilitary arrival on 21 April. Join the chronology and resolution to CNMH by event, date and municipality. Keep the published degree of precision. This is a selected episode suitable for testing source linkage and within-day chronology. It cannot establish coverage of routine clashes. [Victims Unit chronology](https://www.unidadvictimas.gov.co/especiales/Bojaya/index.html) · [Defensoría resolution No. 12](https://www.defensoria.gov.co/documents/20123/1311006/humanitaria12.pdf/76b75942-f491-a828-6df4-406a9e15ed00?download=true&t=1648527337291)
- **Mitú, November 1998. Verified mixed-resolution narrative.** The 60-page Truth Commission case study contains clock estimates and accounts from multiple parties. Examples include a witness giving a 07:00–08:00 interval and a military account dating a movement approximately to 16:00. Record original qualifiers and distinguish retrospective testimony from contemporaneous documents. Its citations to JEP findings and U.S. Embassy cables provide leads to corroborate selected events. The report states that some local police messages were destroyed during the attack, which limits prospects for recovering a continuous original log there. [Truth Commission case study](https://www.comisiondelaverdad.co/sites/default/files/2022-08/Caso%20Toma%20de%20Mitu%CC%81.pdf)
- **Government–FARC–UN monitoring files, 2016–17. Broader two-sided lead.** The public tripartite reports identify cases and parties. The peace agreement describes a national process fed by local and regional documentation. The public samples reviewed here do not establish a recurring subdaily clock. The priority records are original incident intake, field verification and communications logs, with occurrence time distinguished from receipt time. A parallel public lead is CINEP's `Noche y Niebla` narrative case series. Its systematic clock coverage remains unverified. [Tripartite report](https://unmc.unmissions.org/en/node/15178) · [Final agreement](https://apps.procuraduria.gov.co/normograma/compilacion/docs/acuerdo_paz_acuerdopaz_final.htm) · [CINEP Banco de Datos](https://cinep.org.co/publicaciones/sistema-de-informacion-general-sig/)

## Vietnam

- **Daily Staff Journals and Duty Officer's Logs, 1968–70. Verified minute-level log entries.** Downloaded a 100-page scan of the U.S. 2nd Battalion, 35th Infantry S3 journal for 1–15 January 1968. Its journal forms distinguish incoming and outgoing times and include a narrative and action-taken column. The scan's embedded OCR is noisy. Texas Tech catalogues further battalion journals and separately identifies some transcripts. Pilot a contiguous run and extract occurrence time only where the narrative states it. A journal entry time can otherwise serve as a report-time observation. Link by date, place, incident type and unit to SITRA or VNDBA to test clock agreement and look for recorded actions the cleaned series misses. The journals observe one chain of command and cover opposing activity as seen by that chain. [Retrieved January 1968 log](https://cacti35th.com/daily_journals/2-35-1968/68-01-01-15.pdf) · [Texas Tech June 1970 journal](https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu/repositories/2/digital_objects/642188) · [Texas Tech November 1970 transcript listing](https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu/repositories/2/digital_objects/637058)
- **INCDA, 1973–75. Highest-priority unresolved archive.** NARA lists four ASCII-rendered data files and 56 pages of documentation for 26 January 1973–21 April 1975. File-level downloads and the codebook have yet to be obtained. The catalogue describes coverage, but the event clock, time precision and representation of different parties remain unverified. [NARA electronic records finding aid](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/vietnam-war/electronic-data-files) · [INCDA series](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/601815)
- **ICCS and April 1973 ceasefire reports. Complementary sources.** Downloaded the 110-page Nixon Library scan and the UN finding aid. The UN inventory lists five unclassified ICCS folders, `S-0298-0001-01` through `-05`, and marks them unscanned. Field files remain outstanding. Canada's official history describes very uneven complaint and investigation coverage. Keep complaints, independently observed incidents and findings as distinct observations. Sample OCR of eight pages confirmed that some explicit times, including 16:00 EST, are report cutoffs. Sample charts aggregate violations and casualties. These pages do not establish event-level clock coverage. [Nixon Library scan](https://www.nixonlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/2019-07/VIETNAM%20POST%20CEASE-FIRE%20REPORT%20APRIL%201973.pdf) · [UN finding aid](https://search.archives.un.org/downloads/ag-011.pdf) · [Canadian ICCS history](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/military-history/history-heritage/past-operations/asia-pacific/gallant.html)

## Other conflicts

1. **Cyprus, July–August 1974. Strongest new candidate from this search.** UNFICYP is a common observer and names actions by opposing forces. Two retrieved UN PDFs demonstrate incident, report and negotiated times alongside civilian movement, damage to infrastructure and casualties. On 23 July, Add.4 records fighting near Nicosia airport starting at 09:30 and a local ceasefire at 12:30. Add.5 distinguishes a 14:30 agreement, a UN movement at 15:00 and a report of renewed firing at 17:30. Add.4 says UNFICYP logged 29 incidents in the first five hours after the 22 July ceasefire. The public report summarizes that count. Retrieve the underlying logs to see whether the individual records survive and whether the series includes quiet periods and smaller incidents. The public addenda establish a short pilot, while Gaza-like multi-year coverage remains unverified. [S/11353/Add.4](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/484451/files/S_11353_Add.4-EN.pdf) · [S/11353/Add.5](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/484452/files/S_11353_Add.5-EN.pdf) · [UNFICYP history](https://unficyp.unmissions.org/en/node/73113)
2. **Bosnia, Sarajevo, 1994. Strongest candidate already on the project list.** Confirmed the archive pages for Canadian UNPROFOR intelligence volumes 5 and 6, covering 16 January to 28 February. The volume PDFs were not retrieved in this pass. The UN Commission of Experts' Sarajevo chronology combines archival and documentary evidence on shelling, sniping, casualties and damage. ICTY filings identify original UN monitoring reports. The Galić judgment, paragraph 448, attributes a 12:10–12:15 event window on 5 February to the original UN investigation. Use the Sarajevo chronology and cases as indexes into the original monitoring records. Continuous subdaily coverage remains unverified. [Canada Declassified volume 5](https://declassified.library.utoronto.ca/exhibits/show/unprofor-intelligence-reports/volume-5--1994-01-16-to-1994-0) · [Volume 6](https://declassified.library.utoronto.ca/exhibits/show/unprofor-intelligence-reports/volume-6--1994-02-16-to-1994-0) · [UN Sarajevo study](https://dpu-p-001.sitecorecontenthub.cloud/api/public/content/a105e86d069b4bc0b49e0169290b0065?v=0e0827fc) · [Galić judgment](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/galic/tjug/en/gal-tj031205-2.htm) · [Tribunal exhibit selection](https://sarajevo.sensecentar.org/selection-of-exhibits)
3. **Falklands/Malvinas, 1982. Good for cross-checking historical clocks.** The Argentine Ministry of Defence's digitized historical report was retrieved and contains event and message-receipt clocks in the same chronology. For example, it distinguishes the approximate time of the Belgrano sinking attack from the later receipt of a naval message. The Argentine open archive and the British reports of proceedings provide a route to comparing independently recorded times. The short campaign and selected engagement coverage limit its value for estimating a repeated-conflict equilibrium. [Argentine Defence Ministry open archives](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/defensa/archivos-abiertos/malvinas-40-anos) · [Retrieved Argentine report](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/informe_malvinas.pdf) · [British reports of proceedings](https://www.margaretthatcher.org/document/241706)

## First extraction tests

Start with a continuous four-week Cyprus observer-report window if the documents support it, a continuous Vietnam unit-journal run paired to the incident data, and the multi-source Bojayá chronology. For each, count explicitly timed events by party and record type, unresolved same-time events, and hours or days with documented monitoring. Measure the share of clocks that refer to actual occurrence before selecting a sequencing model. Keep dated rows with incomplete clocks available for coverage diagnostics.
